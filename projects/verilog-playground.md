## Project overview

In 2018, I created Verilog Playground, an FPGA board simulator designed to improve Computer Organization and Architecture (COA) classes at my college.

Before the simulator, students depended on access to physical FPGA boards and lab scheduling. This created queues and limited practical experimentation time.

Verilog Playground removed these physical board-access bottlenecks and reduced build/test turnaround from over 3 minutes to about 7 seconds, enabling much faster incremental testing and feedback.

## Impact

- More students could practice outside strict hardware lab windows.
- Faster iteration cycles improved debugging and learning outcomes.
- Instructors could run exercises with less setup friction.
